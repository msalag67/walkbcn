Neighbourhood: La Barceloneta

Barceloneta beach

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing at the edge of one of Barcelona's most distinctive neighbourhoods, and its story starts with a loss. In the 1750s, Barceloneta was built for fishermen and for families who had lost their homes when the Ribera district was demolished. That demolition came after Barcelona fell in 1714, when the new king ordered a huge fortress, the Ciutadella, built on the site of their old streets. The people pushed out needed somewhere to live, and this triangle of land by the port became the answer. Turn around and look at the streets behind the beach. Notice how straight and narrow they are, laid out in long, regular rows. That orderly grid was planned by military engineers, which is very different from the tangled medieval lanes of the old city. The houses were first built low, and more floors were added later, which is why many buildings now stand taller over such slim streets. Inside are tiny flats, and you can see the life in them in the laundry drying on the balconies. These are homes, so enjoy them from a respectful distance. Now face the water. The beach as you see it was largely created for the 1992 Olympics. Before that, the waterfront was cut off by industry and railway lines, and the city mostly lived with its back to the sea. The Olympic project opened up this shoreline, and Barcelona finally turned to face the Mediterranean. Take a moment to look along the sand and imagine how recent all of it really is.

Tip: Wear a top when walking through the neighbourhood streets — it's a local request, and the law.
