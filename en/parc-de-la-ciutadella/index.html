Parc de la Ciutadella

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Remember that fortress built after 1714? You're standing where it stood. After the siege ended, King Philip the Fifth ordered a huge star-shaped citadel raised right here, and for 150 years it loomed over the city as a symbol of occupation. Picture soldiers on the ramparts where people now stroll beneath the trees. When it was finally torn down, the people turned this ground into something the city had never had: its first public park. It was designed by the architect Josep Fontserè, and in 1888 it hosted Barcelona's Universal Exhibition. That's why the red-brick Arc de Triomf stands nearby: it was built as the exhibition's entrance. Now head toward the monumental fountain, with its tumbling water and the golden chariot shining on top. Fontserè designed it, and one of the people who worked on it was a young student called Antoni Gaudí, years before the buildings that made him famous. Look closely at the rockwork and sculptures and picture him there, learning his trade. Some of the old fortress survives, too. The Parliament of Catalonia meets in its former arsenal, inside the park. So the place built to control the city now holds its parliament and its picnics. Today this is where Barcelona plays. Listen for drummers, watch the jugglers, and notice families spreading out on the grass. If you have time, the park's lake is a calm spot to rest your feet. Many of the people around you live just around the corner, so enjoy it the way they do, relaxed and unhurried.

Tip: Rent a rowing boat on the small lake, or just find shade under the palms.
