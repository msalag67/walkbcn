Stop for a moment and take in this giant face. It's the work of Roy Lichtenstein, the American pop artist, and it was made for the 1992 Olympic Games. Lichtenstein became famous for paintings that look like enlarged frames from comic books, with thick black outlines, flat bright colours and rows of little printed dots. Those dots are called Ben-Day dots, a cheap printing trick from old newspapers and comics, and he turned them into his signature. Now walk a little closer. Instead of paint, the face is covered in pieces of broken ceramic. This is trencadís, the technique Gaudí made famous, the same glittering patchwork you may have seen on the benches of Park Güell. Lichtenstein worked on the piece with the ceramicist Diego Delas, and together they set the shards out so they read like those comic-strip dots. From a distance it looks printed; up close it's handmade and slightly uneven, every fragment catching the light in its own way. Follow the bold outlines around the face and notice how simple, flat shapes are enough to suggest a whole personality. So what you're looking at is a meeting of two worlds: American pop art, born from advertising and comics, and Catalan Modernisme, born from craft and nature. It stands right by the old port, at the bottom of Via Laietana, greeting everyone who walks between the city and the water.

Tip: Walk a few steps back towards the sea to see the whole face against the sky.
