Casa Batlló

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing on Passeig de Gràcia, in front of one of Antoni Gaudí's most famous works. Start at the very top. Look at the roof: it rises like the scaly back of a dragon, its glazed ceramic tiles shifting colour as the light changes. Now find the tower, crowned with a cross. That tower is the sword of Sant Jordi, Catalonia's patron saint, plunged into the dragon's back. Sant Jordi is said to have slain a dragon, and Catalans still celebrate him every year on the twenty-third of April, when the city fills with books and roses. Let your eyes come down the facade. The balconies resemble skulls, with their empty eye sockets, and on the lower floors the columns between the windows look like bones. That's why some people call this the House of Bones. Between them, the wall sparkles with fragments of coloured glass and ceramic, a mosaic technique Gaudí loved, called trencadís. Here's the surprising part: this wasn't built from scratch. Gaudí took an ordinary house and rebuilt it for the textile industrialist Josep Batlló, between 1904 and 1906, turning it into a legend in stone and glass. Now look along the street to the neighbouring buildings. This stretch is nicknamed the Block of Discord, because several of Barcelona's great modernista architects built here side by side, each in a strikingly different style. Next door is Casa Amatller, by Josep Puig i Cadafalch. Before you move on, please keep to the side of the pavement. This is a busy street where people live, work and shop every day.

Tip: Next door is Casa Amatller, by a rival architect — this block is nicknamed the Block of Discord.
Enjoy it from outside
Visiting
The façade is free to see from Passeig de Gràcia. The house can be visited with a ticket.
