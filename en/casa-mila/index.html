Casa Milà (La Pedrera)

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Take a moment here on the pavement and look up at this rippling wall of pale stone. When it opened in 1912, locals mocked it. They called it La Pedrera, the stone quarry, and the nickname stuck so well that most people still use it today. Its official name is Casa Milà, after the couple who commissioned it, Pere Milà and his wife Roser Segimon. The architect was Antoni Gaudí, and this was the last private home he designed before giving himself over to the Sagrada Família. Now try to find a straight wall on the façade. Follow the curves around the corner, along each floor, up to the top. You won't find one. The whole front swells and dips like a cliff worn down by the sea. Look next at the balconies. Their wrought iron twists and tangles like seaweed caught on rocks. Then let your eyes travel up to the roofline. From down here you may glimpse the tops of the chimneys and ventilation towers. Up close they look like helmeted warriors standing guard, and some say they inspired the look of Darth Vader. Whether or not that's true, they're among the strangest guardians any rooftop has ever had. Since 1984, it has been on UNESCO's World Heritage List as part of the works of Antoni Gaudí. But it's not only a monument. People still live in some of these apartments, so enjoy the view quietly and leave room on the pavement for neighbours passing by.

Tip: Even from the street, check the iron balconies up close — each one is different.

Visiting
The façade is free to see from the street. The roof and the visit inside need a ticket; people still live in some flats.
