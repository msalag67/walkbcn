Plaça de George Orwell

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing in a small square that carries the name of an English writer, George Orwell. Orwell was a pen name; he was born Eric Blair. In late December 1936 he came to Barcelona to fight for the Republic in the Spanish Civil War. He joined a militia linked to a small party called the POUM and was sent to the Aragon front. Months later, back in the city on leave, he lived through the street fighting of May 1937, when groups on the same Republican side turned their guns on each other, and barricades went up across central Barcelona. Not long after, back at the front, a sniper's bullet hit him in the throat. He survived, and he and his wife Eileen escaped Spain that summer. In 1938 he published Homage to Catalonia, his account of those intense months. Now take a slow look around you. Notice the terraces, the tables close together, the neighbours stopping to chat. Look up at the balconies, where people live their everyday lives above the square, and keep your voice gentle for them. Notice how the narrow streets of the Gothic Quarter feed into this open space, the kind of streets that were easy to block in 1937. The Rambla, where Orwell kept watch from a rooftop during that fighting, is only a few minutes' walk away. Today this is a lively meeting point, a long way from the barricades he wrote about.

Tip: Neighbours live around the square: enjoy the terraces, but keep the noise down at night.
