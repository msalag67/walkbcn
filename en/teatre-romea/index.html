Neighbourhood: El Raval

Teatre Romea

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing on Carrer de l'Hospital, in front of the Teatre Romea, where plays have been staged since 1863. The street takes its name from the old Hospital de la Santa Creu, whose Gothic buildings stand nearby on this same street. The theatre itself is named after Julián Romea, a celebrated Spanish actor of the nineteenth century. For much of its history, the Romea was the home of theatre in Catalan. Generations of Catalan actors and playwrights made their names on this stage, and they kept performing in their own language even in years when it was pushed out of public life. Under the Franco dictatorship, for example, Catalan was heavily restricted in schools, the press and official life, so a stage where it could still be heard mattered a great deal to people here. Now look at the entrance and the posters beside the doors. They tell you what's on right now, because the Romea is a working theatre, not a museum. It still presents modern productions, mostly in Catalan, for a local audience. If you pass by in the evening before a performance, you may see people from the neighbourhood and across the city greeting friends before the doors open. Notice, too, how modestly the building sits in the everyday street front of El Raval. People live all around here, so as you take it in, keep your voice low and leave the doorway clear for them.

Tip: Check the programme: some productions offer surtitles.
