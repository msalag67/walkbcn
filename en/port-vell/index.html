Neighbourhood: Port Vell

Port Vell

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

This is Port Vell, Barcelona's old port, where the city met the sea for centuries. For most of that time this was a place of work, with ships loading and unloading along the quays. Right in front of you is the Rambla de Mar, a wooden walkway stretching out over the water. It isn't fixed in place: it swings open to let boats pass, so keep an eye on it and you might catch it moving. Follow it with your eyes and you'll see it leads across the harbour to the Maremagnum shopping centre on the far side. Now turn and look back towards the city. At the end of the Rambla stands the Columbus monument, a tall column with a statue of Christopher Columbus on top. It was built for the Universal Exhibition of 1888 and rises around sixty metres. Notice his raised arm: he's pointing out to sea, across the Mediterranean. Near the foot of the monument, look for the long stone halls of the Drassanes, the medieval royal shipyards where galleys were once built. Today they house the city's Maritime Museum. Since the 1992 Olympics, this old working harbour has become a promenade for walking, cycling and watching the boats. Along the waterfront lies Barceloneta, the old fishermen's neighbourhood, and rising above the harbour is the green hill of Montjuïc. Before you move on, stand still for a moment and watch the masts sway, just as people have watched them here for centuries.

Tip: It's a lovely place at sunset: find a bench and watch the boats come in.
