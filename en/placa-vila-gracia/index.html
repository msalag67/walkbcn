Neighbourhood: Gràcia

Plaça de la Vila de Gràcia

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You are standing in what was once the heart of a whole town. Before 1897, Gràcia was not part of Barcelona at all. It was an independent town with its own council, and this square was where its public life came together. Look around and you can still feel that it is the centre of something. Now look up at the slender clock tower rising from the square. It was built in the 1860s, while Gràcia still governed itself, and it was designed by the architect Antoni Rovira i Trias, the same man whose plan for Barcelona's grand expansion won a city competition, only to be set aside for Ildefons Cerdà's. Notice how tall and narrow the tower is, so you can spot it from almost every corner. Over time it has become the symbol of the neighbourhood. Opposite stands Gràcia's district hall. The building was designed by Francesc Berenguer, a close collaborator of Antoni Gaudí, and it is still where the district is run, so the square keeps the civic role it had when Gràcia was its own town. In August, during the Festa Major, giant figures dance here and the whole square fills with music. That same week, residents decorate their own streets, and neighbours compete to create the most imaginative ones. Whatever the season, remember that people live in the flats all around you. Find a spot, keep your voice low, and enjoy the square the way they do.

Tip: Neighbours live all around the square: in the evening, keep the noise down.
