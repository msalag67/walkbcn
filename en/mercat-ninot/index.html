Neighbourhood: Eixample

Mercat del Ninot

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Ninot means a little figure or doll in Catalan, and it's a charming name for a food market. It comes from a carved wooden figurehead, the kind that once sat on the bow of a sailing ship. That figurehead once hung outside a nearby tavern, and the market took its name from it. Take a moment to look at the building itself. What you see is modern, rebuilt in the 2010s. Now glance at the streets around you. This is the Eixample, the grid of blocks planned by the engineer Ildefons Cerdà in the nineteenth century, and you'll notice the corners of the blocks are cut off at an angle rather than square. That gives the crossings their open, roomy feel, and it's one of the easiest ways to know you're in this part of the city. Stand on one of those corners and you get a wide view of the whole block. If you head inside, you'll find out why the market has its reputation. Local food lovers come here above all for the fish and seafood, laid out fresh on the counters, and for good-quality produce. Notice how people shop: many chat with the stallholders, ask what's best today, and buy just what they need. If you'd like to buy something, a simple 'bon dia', good morning in Catalan, goes a long way. Remember this is a working neighbourhood market, not a showpiece. Keep to the side of the aisles, ask before taking close-up photos of stalls or people, and let the regulars get on with their shopping.

Tip: Mornings are best: that's when the stalls are full and the neighbourhood does its shopping.
