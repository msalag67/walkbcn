Plaça Reial

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Welcome to Plaça Reial, one of the most elegant squares in Barcelona, tucked just off La Rambla. Before you take in the details, picture what stood here before: a convent belonging to the Capuchin friars. In the 19th century that land was cleared, and this arcaded square was built in its place, designed by the architect Francesc Daniel Molina as one harmonious whole. Notice how the same arches repeat all the way around, and how the buildings above them match, floor by floor, so the square feels closed off and calm, almost like a courtyard. Now look at the tall iron lampposts. Each one spreads six curling arms, and on top sits a helmet with wings. That is the winged helmet of Mercury, the Roman god of commerce, a nod to a city that lived by trade. These lampposts were among the first public works of a young Antoni Gaudí, commissioned by the city long before anyone had heard of the Sagrada Família. If you look closely, you can already sense his love of curves and ornament. In the middle, the fountain is known as the Fountain of the Three Graces. The palm trees around it soften all those straight lines and give the square its relaxed, almost southern feel. From morning coffee to late at night, the cafés under the arches turn this place into an outdoor living room. People live in the flats above, so enjoy it the way they do: unhurried, and kindly.

Tip: People live in the flats above the arcades: at night, enjoy the square quietly.
