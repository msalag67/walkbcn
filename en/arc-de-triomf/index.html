Neighbourhood: Sant Pere

Arc de Triomf

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

This red-brick arch was not built to celebrate a battle. It was the grand entrance to the 1888 Universal Exhibition, Barcelona's first world fair, and its architect was Josep Vilaseca. The city wanted to show the world it was modern, confident and open to visitors, and this arch was the first thing many of them saw. Start with the material. Rather than marble or stone, Vilaseca used red brick, and he laid it in Moorish-style patterns, a nod to Spain's Islamic heritage. This style is often called Neo-Mudéjar, and it was fashionable in Spain at the time. Now look at the carved frieze on the side facing Passeig de Sant Joan. It was made by the sculptor Josep Reynés, and it shows Barcelona welcoming the nations who came to the fair. Walk through and turn around, and on the park side you'll find a second frieze by Josep Llimona, on the theme of the awards given out at the exhibition. Near the top, look for the shields of Spain's provinces, crowned by Barcelona's own coat of arms. Then follow the wide promenade, Passeig de Lluís Companys, lined with palm trees and tall, ornate street lamps. It leads straight to Parc de la Ciutadella, where the exhibition itself was held. People live in the buildings all around you, so enjoy the view quietly and keep the pavement clear for neighbours on their way home.

Tip: Walk down the promenade towards Ciutadella: it's one of the most pleasant short walks in the city.
