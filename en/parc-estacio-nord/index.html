Parc de l'Estació del Nord

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing on old railway land. Trains once ran across this ground beside the former North Station, and later the space became a park for the neighbourhood. The big station building is still there, and today it's Barcelona's main long-distance bus station. Now look at the ground. Those long, wave-like forms covered in blue ceramic are a land-art work by the American artist Beverly Pepper, called Fallen Sky, or Cel Caigut in Catalan. It's as if a piece of the sky had dropped into the grass. Step a little closer and look at the surface: it's a mosaic of broken ceramic pieces in many shades of blue, a technique Barcelona knows well from Gaudí. Notice how the colour shifts when clouds pass, and how the curves seem to rise and sink as you walk slowly around them. Pepper also designed another piece in this park, a spiral of trees called Wooded Spiral, so keep an eye out for it as you wander. Most of all, watch how the people who live here use this space. Children climb the ceramic waves, dogs run around them and neighbours read in the sun. Nobody treats it like a museum piece. Sit on a bench for a minute and simply watch. Art here is something you walk on, not just look at. If you join in, do it gently, the way the locals do, because this is their everyday park.

Tip: A quiet, little-known park: perfect for a break away from the crowds.
