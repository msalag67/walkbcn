Neighbourhood: El Guinardó

Recinte Modernista de Sant Pau

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

This looks like a fairytale town, but it was a working hospital right up until 2009. When patients moved out, they didn't go far: hospital care continues in a modern building just to the north of these old pavilions. The story begins with a gift. A Barcelona banker named Pau Gil left money in his will for a new hospital, and the architect chosen was Lluís Domènech i Montaner. He believed beauty helped people heal, so instead of one huge block he spread the hospital across separate pavilions, each surrounded by gardens and filled with light. Look at the rooftops and domes around you, covered in coloured tiles, and at the warm exposed brick, carved stone and mosaic details on the walls. Nothing here was meant to feel cold or frightening. Now notice the way the site sits. The whole complex is turned at an angle to the neat street grid of the Eixample, and Domènech i Montaner chose that orientation so the wards would catch better sun and air. Then picture what you can't see. Underneath the gardens, a network of tunnels linked the buildings, moving patients and supplies out of sight, so the paths above stayed peaceful places to rest and recover. Domènech i Montaner didn't live to see it all finished; his son Pere completed the work. Today the site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. As you wander, remember that people live all around this neighbourhood, so enjoy it calmly and quietly.

Tip: Sagrada Família is a straight 10-minute walk down Avinguda de Gaudí.
