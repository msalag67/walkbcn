Neighbourhood: El Raval

MACBA

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing in front of MACBA, Barcelona's Museum of Contemporary Art, and that bright white building is hard to miss. It was designed by the American architect Richard Meier and opened in 1995, dropped right into the Raval, one of the city's oldest and most tightly packed neighbourhoods. Look at the narrow streets around you, the balconies and the old façades, and then back at this clean white block. That contrast is a big part of the story here. Meier, who won the Pritzker Prize, architecture's best-known award, in 1984, is famous for working almost entirely in white, so the building changes with the light as the day goes on. The square you're in is the Plaça dels Àngels, and what happened here was never part of the plan. The smooth stone, the ledges and the gentle slopes turned out to be perfect for skateboarding, and over the years this became one of the most famous skate spots in Europe. Skaters from around the world know it from videos and photos, and some travel here just to ride it. So stand for a minute and simply listen. That clack, roll and scrape of wheels on stone is the soundtrack of this place. You might hear a trick land, or miss, again and again. Notice too the older building on the square. That's the former Convent dels Àngels, which gave the square its name. Remember that people live in the flats all around you, so enjoy the show quietly.

Tip: Respect the skaters' space, and wander the Raval's side streets for small independent galleries.
