Neighbourhood: Poble-sec

Carrer de Blai

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing on Carrer de Blai, a pedestrian street in Poble-sec, and it's lined from end to end with pintxo bars. A pintxo is a small bite on a slice of bread, held together with a toothpick. The word comes from the Spanish for 'to pierce', and that little stick is the whole idea. The tradition comes from the Basque Country, in the north of Spain, and here it suits an evening stroll perfectly. Look through the open doors at the counters, with rows of bites waiting, all within reach. Grab what you like straight from the bar, and when you've finished, the staff count your toothpicks to make the bill. So hold on to every one. Many people eat a bite or two in one bar, then move on to the next. Now take a moment to look past the food. Poble-sec was a working-class neighbourhood, built for the people who worked at the port and in the theatres of the nearby Paral·lel, a broad avenue once famous for its music halls and cabarets. The neighbourhood sits at the foot of Montjuïc, the hill overlooking the port. Look up above the bar signs at the balconies. Those are homes, and this is still a street where people live, so keep your voice gentle as you go. Even the neighbourhood's name is local. Poble-sec means 'dry village' in Catalan. Enjoy the bites, and enjoy the street.

Tip: Weekday early evenings are more relaxed — and keep it quiet, people live upstairs.
Enjoy it from outsideHomes all around: keep voices low
What to look at
The pintxos on the counters, and the balconies above the bars: people live here.
Where to listen
At the wide end by Plaça del Sortidor, not in front of doorways. Better early evening, and not as a big group.
