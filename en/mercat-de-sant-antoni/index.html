Neighbourhood: Sant Antoni

Mercat de Sant Antoni

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Stand back for a moment and take in the size of this place. The Mercat de Sant Antoni fills a whole block of the Eixample, the grid of streets laid out when Barcelona finally spread beyond its old walls. It first opened in 1882, designed by the architect Antoni Rovira i Trias, and its iron and glass frame is a fine example of the great covered markets of that time. Seen from above, the building forms a huge cross. After nine years of restoration, it reopened in 2018, and those works brought a surprise. As they dug, builders uncovered part of the 17th-century city wall, the very kind of barrier the Eixample was built to escape. Rather than bury it again, they left it on view in the basement, so you can walk down and see the old stonework lying beneath a working market. Before you go in, look up at the ironwork and the long sweep of the roofs. If you're here on a Sunday morning, the outer ring changes character completely. Stalls of second-hand books, comics and stamps line the outside, and collectors have been meeting here to swap and bargain since the 1930s. Watch the regulars flicking through boxes with practised hands, or leaning over albums of stamps, deep in conversation. Whatever day it is, remember this is still the neighbourhood's market. Keep to the side when it's busy, and let people get on with their shopping.

Tip: Come on a Sunday morning for the book market, then join the locals for vermut nearby.
