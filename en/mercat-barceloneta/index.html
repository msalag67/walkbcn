Neighbourhood: La Barceloneta

Mercat de la Barceloneta

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing in front of the market of La Barceloneta, the old fishermen's neighbourhood by the sea. There has been a market on this spot since the 19th century, and it's still where people here do their everyday shopping. Start by looking up. That bold roof seems to float above the building, almost as if it were hovering rather than resting. It was added in 2007, when the market was renovated by the architect Josep Miàs, and it carries solar panels, so the roof that shelters the market also gathers energy from the sun. Now lower your eyes to the older layer beneath it: the iron structure of the original market. The architects kept that iron frame rather than replacing it, so you're looking at two eras at once, the old market below and the new roof above. As you walk around, notice the streets leading away from the square. They're narrow and straight, laid out in a tight grid. That's because Barceloneta was planned in the 18th century to rehouse families who lost their homes when a military fortress was built nearby. Over time it became the home of fishermen and port workers. Inside the market, the sea still sets the tone. Look for the fish and seafood counters, then for the small bars tucked among the stalls, where locals sit at the counter for lunch. If you go in, remember this is a working neighbourhood market, not a stage. Wait your turn, ask before taking photos of stallholders, and keep your voice down around the homes that line the square.

Tip: Most Barcelona markets close on Sundays and many stalls close in the afternoon.
