Plaça de Sant Jaume

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Stand in the middle of the square and turn slowly all the way around. On one side is the Palau de la Generalitat, home of the Catalan government. Directly facing it is Barcelona's City Hall. Two governments, looking at each other across one open space. Few places put power so plainly face to face. Take a closer look at the Generalitat first. Its front on this square is in a sober Renaissance style. Above the entrance, in a niche, stands Saint George, patron saint of Catalonia, the dragon-slayer you'll meet all over this city. Now turn to City Hall. The front you see here is neoclassical, added much later, but walk round the corner onto Carrer de la Ciutat and you'll find its older Gothic façade tucked into the side street. None of this is new ground. This was already the heart of the city in Roman times, when the forum of Barcino stood here, close to where the town's two main streets crossed. Just a few steps away, on Carrer del Paradís, four tall columns survive from the Roman temple of Augustus, standing inside a building's courtyard. The square itself takes its name from a church of Saint James that once stood here. Today it's still where people gather to celebrate and to protest, and on festival days to watch teams of castellers build human towers between the two façades. People live in the lanes all around, so as you head off exploring, keep your voice low.

Tip: Walk round the corner into Carrer del Bisbe to see Sant Jordi carved on the Generalitat's Gothic façade.
