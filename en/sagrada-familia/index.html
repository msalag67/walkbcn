Sagrada Família

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

In 1882, builders started work right here on a modest neo-Gothic church. The first architect was Francisco de Paula del Villar. A year later, the project passed to a young architect named Antoni Gaudí, and he did something bold: he threw out the plan and started again. He spent the next 43 years on it. In his final years he gave up other commissions and devoted himself to this church alone, and near the end he was living in his workshop on the site. People kept asking why it was taking so long. His answer has become famous: my client is not in a hurry. He was talking about God. Gaudí died in 1926, after being struck by a tram, and he is buried in the crypt beneath the building. The work carried on for generations after him, paid for largely by donations and, these days, by visitors' tickets. Now look up at the columns. They don't rise straight like ordinary pillars. They lean and branch, splitting the way a tree does, because Gaudí wanted the inside to feel like a forest. Let your eye follow one from the base to where it divides, and see if you can spot where trunk becomes branch. Before you move on, step aside from doorways and keep the pavement clear. People live and work all around here, and this is their neighbourhood too.

Tip: Walk around to the Passion façade side — it's calmer and the sculpture is stark and striking.
Enjoy it from outsidePlace of worship: quiet, and no visits during services
Visiting
The façades are free to see from the street. Going inside needs a timed ticket, usually booked ahead.
