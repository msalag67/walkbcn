Plaça del Pi

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

This little square takes its name from a pine tree. Legend talks of a palm and a pine on this spot, and by the 16th century a pine grew here so tall that it could be seen above the houses. The one you see now was planted in 1985 to keep the tradition alive. Behind it rises the Gothic church of Santa Maria del Pi, which gave its name to the whole neighbourhood. Now turn around. Opposite the church stands the old house of a guild of resellers: look at its façade, decorated with patterns scratched into the plaster, a technique called sgraffito. On the corner with Carrer del Cardenal Casañas, the Casa de la Confraria de la Sang is an example of the city's houses from the early 17th century. Three streets meet here, Petritxol, del Pi and Cardenal Casañas, and this is one of the places where people who live in the old town cross paths every day, on their way to work, school or the shops. Take a moment on the side of the square and simply watch how it works.

Tip: People live above the terraces and the church is in use: keep your voice down and leave the church steps clear.
Enjoy it from outsideHomes all around: keep voices lowPlace of worship: quiet, and no visits during services
What to look at
The pine tree, the Gothic church behind it, and opposite it the sgraffito façade of the old guild house.
Where to listen
Beside the pine, at the edge of the square, away from the terrace tables and the church door.
Visiting
A public square, always open. The church opens for services and, at set times, for visits: check before going in.
