Neighbourhood: El Raval

CCCB

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing in the courtyard of the CCCB, Barcelona's Centre for Contemporary Culture. These walls once belonged to the Casa de la Caritat, a former charity house that sheltered the city's poor. Look around at the stone, the long rows of windows, the sober lines. Then find the one side that breaks the pattern. When the building was renovated, the architects, Albert Viaplana and Helio Piñón, cut that side of the courtyard away and put up a tall glass wall instead. The cultural centre opened here in 1994. Notice that the wall doesn't stand straight. Near the top it leans forward, out over the courtyard, as if it's bending down to look at you. Tilt your head back and follow it up. Because of that lean, the glass works like a giant mirror, showing you the rooftops of the Raval and the hills behind the city, views you can't see directly from down here. Stay with it for a moment. A passing cloud or a shift in the sun, and the whole picture changes. On a grey day it turns soft and silvery; in bright light the reflection sharpens. That's the quiet trick of this place: old charity house and new glass side by side, each making the other easier to see. The centre hosts exhibitions, talks and festivals all year round, so it's worth checking what's on before you move on. And just around the corner is MACBA, the Museum of Contemporary Art, with its bright white building and a square that skateboarders love.

Tip: The courtyard is free to enter and often has outdoor events in summer.
