Neighbourhood: El Raval

Drassanes Reials

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

These huge stone halls were the royal shipyards of Barcelona, the Drassanes Reials. In the Middle Ages, galleys were built here for the fleet of the Crown of Aragon, and work on the yards began in the late thirteenth century, with major expansions in the century that followed. Step back now and take in the long rows of Gothic arches. They were made wide and high for a very practical reason: so that whole ships could be built under cover, out of the sun and rain. Let your eye travel along them and notice how they repeat, bay after bay, giving the building its calm, steady rhythm. Then look at the sheer weight of the stone. This was a workplace, not a palace or a church, yet it was built to last for centuries, and it has. It's considered one of the finest medieval industrial buildings in the world. It also sat right at the edge of the medieval city, close to the sea, and a stretch of the old city wall still survives alongside the complex. Today the shipyards house the Maritime Museum. Inside, under those same arches, you can see a full-size replica of the Real, the flagship of the Holy League fleet at the Battle of Lepanto in 1571. Standing beside it gives you a real sense of how much space a galley needed, and why these halls are so vast. Before you move on, take one last look along the arches and picture the ships slowly taking shape beneath them.

Tip: Look at the arches from the entrance hall even if you don't visit the museum.
