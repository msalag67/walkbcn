El Molino

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Find the windmill on the façade above you. Its sails mark El Molino, Barcelona's most famous cabaret, open since the early twentieth century and inspired by the Moulin Rouge in Paris, the Montmartre cabaret that opened in 1889 under its own red windmill. Barcelona borrowed the idea and made it its own. Now turn and look along the avenue. This is the Avinguda del Paral·lel, and its name has a neat story behind it: the street roughly follows a line of latitude, a parallel, across the map of the city. A century ago it was Barcelona's Broadway. Picture it lined with theatres, music halls and cafés, lights glowing, doors open, crowds spilling onto the pavement. Most of the audiences were working people, who came here after a hard week to laugh, sing along and forget their troubles for a few hours. Over the decades, most of those halls closed, one after another, and the avenue changed around them. El Molino survived. It still puts on shows today, so the windmill above you isn't just a souvenir of the past but the sign of a working stage. Take a moment, too, to notice the green slopes of Montjuïc hill close by. The neighbourhood at its foot is Poble-sec, home to families who have lived here for generations. So as you explore, keep your voice low on the residential side streets, and enjoy the avenue the way the locals do: slowly, and without any rush.

Tip: People live above the theatres on the Paral·lel: keep it calm when shows let out.
