Neighbourhood: Eixample

Fundació Antoni Tàpies

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Look up at the roof. That tangle of metal wire floating against the sky is a sculpture called Cloud and Chair, by Antoni Tàpies, one of Catalonia's most important twentieth-century artists. Tàpies was born here in Barcelona in 1923, and he set up this foundation to share his work with the public. The sculpture went up on the roof in 1990, when the foundation opened its doors. Now bring your eyes down to the building it sits on. This red-brick façade was designed by the Modernista architect Lluís Domènech i Montaner, the same architect who later created the Palau de la Música Catalana. It was built in the 1880s as the home of the Montaner i Simon publishing house, a firm with ties to the architect's family. Notice how the brick is left bare rather than hidden under stone or paint, and how it is combined with iron, a bold choice at the time. Historians see it as one of the early buildings of the Modernista movement in Barcelona. So in one glance you are taking in two very different moments: a nineteenth-century workplace made for printing books, and a late twentieth-century artwork that turns the roofline into a kind of drawing in the air. Old and new art meet here, one on top of the other. Take your time looking, but keep to the edge of the pavement. This is an ordinary Eixample street, and the people walking past you live and work right here.

Tip: Even without entering, the façade and the rooftop sculpture are worth a pause.
