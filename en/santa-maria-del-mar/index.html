Neighbourhood: El Born

Santa Maria del Mar

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing in front of what people here call the cathedral of the people. It isn't actually Barcelona's cathedral, which is a few streets away in the Gothic Quarter, but this church was raised by the neighbourhood itself. Work began in 1329, and by 1383 it was finished. That's just 54 years, astonishingly fast for a Gothic church of this size. The dock workers and fishermen of La Ribera, the old waterfront district, gave their labour. And the bastaixos, the porters of the harbour, carried every stone on their backs from the quarry at Montjuïc, the hill above the port. Picture them making that walk again and again, block after block. Now look at the main doors. Find the small carved figures of those porters, still at work. Look up, too, at the great round rose window above the entrance. When you step inside, the mood changes completely. The columns are so slender, and the side aisles rise so nearly to the height of the central nave, that the space feels weightless, almost empty. Part of that bareness has a sad history. In 1936, at the start of the Spanish Civil War, a fire burned inside for days and destroyed most of the decoration. What survived is the pure stone structure, and many people think it's more beautiful for it. Before you go in, remember that this is still El Born's parish church, with weddings, masses and neighbours who come to pray. Keep your voice low and your camera discreet.

Tip: Entry to the basilica is usually free at set times — check the door.
