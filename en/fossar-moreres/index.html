Fossar de les Moreres

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing in the Fossar de les Moreres, and the name tells you what this place was. Fossar is Catalan for graveyard, and moreres are mulberry trees. This small, sloping square beside Santa Maria del Mar was the church's burial ground, and it became the resting place of defenders who died during the siege of Barcelona, a siege that ended on 11 September 1714. Turn toward the church and take in its side wall: this Gothic basilica was raised in the fourteenth century, and it has watched over this ground ever since. Now find the inscription. It says that no traitor is buried here. Those words come from a poem by the nineteenth-century Catalan writer Frederic Soler, better known as Pitarra, and they turned a simple cemetery into a statement of loyalty. Next, look at the red arch and the eternal flame that burns there. Together they make this a place of memory rather than just a square. Every 11 September, Catalonia's national day, people gather here to remember. If you want to see more of what that year changed, walk to the end of Passeig del Born, where the Born cultural centre shows the remains of streets demolished after the siege to make room for a fortress. Before you go, notice the doors and balconies around you. People live here, so speak softly and leave the space as calm as you found it.

Tip: It's a memorial, not a meeting point: keep it calm and respectful.
