Recinte de l'Escola Industrial

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Look at the long brick buildings around you, laid out like streets. In the late 1860s this was the textile factory of the Batlló family, designed by the architect Rafael Guastavino, at a time when the new Eixample was growing and Barcelona was filling up with mills and chimneys. The factory did not last long: it closed towards the end of the 19th century. At the beginning of the 20th century, a public board bought the whole site to create the Escola Industrial, a school to train the engineers, technicians and skilled workers that industrial Catalonia needed. That decision shaped the place you see now: instead of being demolished, the factory became a campus of almost sixty thousand square metres. Look for the clock that gives one of the buildings its name, the Edifici del Rellotge. Some university schools began here before moving to bigger campuses, and today the site, owned by the Diputació de Barcelona, still houses schools, laboratories and a student residence. And the architect? Rafael Guastavino later emigrated to the United States, where his tile vaults became famous. Here, though, his brick factory is still doing what the school set out to do: teaching.

Tip: It is a working campus: stay on the open paths, don't go into the buildings, and keep your voice down near the classrooms.

What to look at
The rows of brick factory buildings, laid out like streets, and the clock of the Edifici del Rellotge.
Where to listen
Just inside the gate at Carrer del Comte d'Urgell, 187, to the side of the path, without blocking the entrance. If the gate is closed, on the pavement outside.
Visiting
The central paths are usually open as a walk-through during the day; the buildings are for students and staff only. Opening of the gates is not guaranteed.
