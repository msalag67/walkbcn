Barcelona Cathedral

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Stand in the square in front of the cathedral and take in the whole front at once. This cathedral took more than six hundred years to finish. Work began in 1298, on the site of earlier churches, and the main façade you're looking at was only completed in the late 1800s. So those pointed arches and lacy pinnacles are younger than they seem, built to match the medieval church behind them. Its full name is the Cathedral of the Holy Cross and Saint Eulàlia, and Eulàlia is the key to this place. She was a young girl martyred in Roman times, and she was Barcelona's original patron saint. Her remains rest in the crypt beneath the main altar, so if you go inside, look for the steps leading down in front of the altar. Then find your way to the cloister, the quiet courtyard built around a garden, and listen. You'll hear geese before you see them. There are always thirteen, one for each year of Eulàlia's life. Watch them for a moment as they patrol the garden. They seem very sure this courtyard belongs to them. Notice too how the noise of the city drops away once you're inside these walls, with palm trees overhead and water trickling from the fountain. Remember this is still a working church, where locals come to pray, so keep your voice low and your phone discreet. When you step back outside, the lanes around you are home to families, so carry that same quiet with you.

Tip: On Sunday mornings locals dance the sardana in the square in front.
