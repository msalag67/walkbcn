Palau de la Música Catalana

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing in front of the Palau de la Música Catalana, built in 1908 for a working-class choir, the Orfeó Català. Its members were ordinary working people who came together to sing, and this hall was built as their home. That's why the whole building is a celebration of Catalan identity, from its brick to its mosaics. Take a moment to look up at the façade. You'll find carved busts of great composers, among them Bach, Beethoven and Wagner, gazing down on the street. On the corner, a sculpture bursts out of the stone. It's a tribute to Catalan folk song, the music these singers loved, and if you look to the very top you'll spot Saint George, Catalonia's patron saint. Notice too the columns on the upper balcony, wrapped in colourful mosaic that seems almost to flower. Inside, the surprise is light. An enormous stained-glass skylight hangs above the audience and glows like a sun. During the day, this is the only concert hall in Europe lit entirely by natural light. Around the stage, sculpted muses emerge from the wall behind the musicians, half in mosaic and half in relief, as if the music itself had taken shape. In 1997 the Palau was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site, but it's still very much a working concert hall, and these narrow streets are where people live. So please keep to the side, leave room for neighbours passing by, and enjoy the view quietly.

Tip: Look for the mosaic columns on the corner — each has a different floral pattern.
