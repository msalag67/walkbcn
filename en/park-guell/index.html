Park Güell

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Welcome to Park Güell. Before it was a park, this hillside was meant to be something quite different: a luxury housing estate for sixty wealthy families, commissioned by the industrialist Eusebi Güell, Gaudí's great patron. Notice that the name uses the English spelling of park. Güell admired the garden cities of Britain, and he hoped to build one here, with elegant homes looking out over the city to the sea. It never took off. Only two houses were ever sold, and one of them went to Gaudí himself, who lived here for many years. His pink house with its slender spire, now the Gaudí House Museum, stands along the paths nearby. The project failed, but the city gained something better. Barcelona later bought the land and opened it as a public park, and today it's part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Now find the famous serpentine bench winding around the main terrace. It was designed to fit the curve of a seated body, so sit back and test it. Legend says a worker sat naked in wet plaster so the builders could copy the shape just right. True or not, you can feel how it holds you. Run your hand over the colourful tiles, then look out past the rooftops toward the sea. And remember that the streets below are home to the people of Gràcia, so keep your voice gentle and enjoy the view.

Tip: Book a timed ticket for the monumental zone — the forest paths above are free.
