Neighbourhood: Port Vell

Moll de la Fusta

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You are on the Moll de la Fusta, the timber wharf, part of the Port Vell, the oldest part of Barcelona's harbour, in use for more than four centuries. For a long time this edge of the city was a working quay, cut off from the streets behind it by sheds, warehouses and traffic: the old town lived with its back to the sea. In the 1980s, as the city began to open up its waterfront, the architect Manuel de Solà-Morales redesigned this wharf as a promenade on several levels, with palm trees, benches and room to walk by the water, while the traffic was kept apart. Look to the south-west and you will see the Columbus monument at the bottom of the Rambla; look the other way and you will find the big ceramic face of the Barcelona Head. In front of you, sailing boats and old ships are moored where cargo used to be unloaded. It is one of the easiest places in the old town to feel the sea, so take a seat, look at the water and let the noise of the city fall behind you.

Tip: Runners, cyclists and families share this promenade: walk on the pedestrian side and don't stop in the cycle lane.
Enjoy it from outside
What to look at
The palm-lined promenade, the boats moored at the quay, the Columbus monument at one end and the Barcelona Head at the other.
Where to listen
On a bench facing the water, out of the cycle lane.
Visiting
A public promenade, open at all times.
