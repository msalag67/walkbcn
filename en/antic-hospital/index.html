Neighbourhood: El Raval

Antic Hospital de la Santa Creu

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing at one of Barcelona's great survivors. This Gothic building was founded in 1401, when the city brought several smaller medieval hospitals together under one roof, and for more than five hundred years it was Barcelona's main hospital. Look at the walls around you: thick, plain stone, built for work rather than show. Inside, the old wards are long halls spanned by tall pointed arches, and for centuries the city's sick lay beneath them. Now picture June 1926. An old man, crossing the city on foot, is struck by a tram. His clothes are so worn and plain that people take him for a poor stranger, and nobody recognises him at first. He is brought here, to the hospital that cared for the city's poor. Only later does it become clear who he is: Antoni Gaudí, the architect of the Sagrada Família. He dies here a few days later, and today he lies buried in the crypt of the church he gave his final years to. By then the hospital's time here was ending. Its work moved to a new site, the Hospital de la Santa Creu i Sant Pau, and these old halls found a quieter purpose. Today they hold the National Library of Catalonia, so where patients once rested, readers now sit with books. Before you go, wander into the courtyard. Orange trees grow among the old arcades, and the noise of El Raval fades. Neighbours cut through here every day and readers come to study, so keep your voice low and enjoy one of the calmest corners of the city.

Tip: The courtyard is free and shady: a perfect place to rest your feet.
