Neighbourhood: Gothic Quarter

Plaça de Sant Felip Neri

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

This is one of the quietest corners of the old town, and one of the saddest. Take a moment before you look up. Notice how the noise of the busy streets drops away here, how the small fountain in the middle softens everything. Now look at the church façade. It is pitted with holes, some shallow, some deep enough to put your fingers in. They are scars from a bombing raid on the thirtieth of January 1938, during the Spanish Civil War. The planes were Italian, flying in support of Franco's forces, and Barcelona was being bombed from the air again and again that year. Bombs fell on this square and killed dozens of people, many of them children who had come here seeking shelter. A second bomb fell while people were trying to help the wounded. The church itself dates from the eighteenth century, but it is these marks from one winter morning that people remember. They were left on purpose, so that no one forgets. Look for the plaque nearby that remembers the victims. Then look around you. There is still a school on this square, and on a weekday you may hear children's voices echoing off the same walls. People live in the flats above you, too. So if you stay a while, keep your voice low and give the square its quiet. It is a beautiful place, and it is also a place of mourning. Both things are true here at once.
