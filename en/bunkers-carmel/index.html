Neighbourhood: El Carmel

Turó de la Rovira (Bunkers del Carmel)

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing on top of the Turó de la Rovira, and from here you can see almost the whole city, from the sea to the mountains. Take a moment to trace it. Find the Mediterranean shining along the horizon. Then turn toward the wooded hills of Collserola, with the church of Tibidabo at the top. Off to one side, Montjuïc sits low beside the port, and closer in you may spot the spires of the Sagrada Família rising above the rooftops. That wide view is exactly why anti-aircraft guns were placed here during the Spanish Civil War. Barcelona was bombed again and again from the air, and from this hilltop gunners could watch the sky and defend the city below. Look at the concrete platforms around you. Some of them held the guns, and you can still make out their shape. The story didn't stop when the war ended. In the hard years that followed, families who had nowhere else to live built shacks among the ruins, using the old military structures as walls and floors. People cooked, raised children and made a community up here, and they lived on this hilltop until 1990. Today the concrete platforms are what remains, and the site is looked after as part of the city's history museum. As you sit and take in the view, remember that the slopes below are still a living neighbourhood. El Carmel is full of people's homes, so keep your voice gentle on the way down.

Tip: A heritage site above people's homes: no parties or music, take your rubbish with you, and keep voices down on the way up.

What to look at
The concrete platforms of the 1938 anti-aircraft battery, and the city from the sea to Collserola.
Where to listen
On the platforms at the top, away from the houses on the way up. Not with music or as a group at night.
Visiting
Free, open-air heritage site of the MUHBA (City History Museum). Its small exhibition spaces open Wed, Fri, Sat and Sun: 10–14 h from October to May, 16–19 h from June to September. Steep uphill walk; not accessible with reduced mobility.
