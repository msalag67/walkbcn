Neighbourhood: Gràcia

Plaça de la Virreina

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing in Plaça de la Virreina, a small, quiet square lined with trees, and one of Gràcia's favourite places for a coffee in the shade. Its name, Virreina, means 'vicereine'. Take a moment to get your bearings. At one end stands the church of Sant Joan, which is Catalan for Saint John. Its front closes off the square and gives your eye somewhere to rest. The church was rebuilt after it was burned during the Civil War, so the building in front of you is a survivor of one of the hardest chapters in the city's history. It also helps to know that Gràcia wasn't always part of Barcelona. It was an independent town until the city annexed it in 1897, and you can still feel that small-town character in its narrow streets and its many little squares. Now turn your back to the church and watch how this square works. Parents push prams across the paving. Older neighbours settle onto the benches. Friends linger on the café terraces, in no particular hurry. That's why people say Gràcia's squares are its living rooms: this is where the neighbourhood meets, chats and passes the time. Every August, during Gràcia's Festa Major, residents decorate many of the neighbourhood's streets themselves, and the whole district turns into a party. Today you're seeing the everyday version, which is just as telling. Keep your voice low, remember that people really live in the buildings all around you, and if you have a few minutes, take a seat and join in.

Tip: It's a calm residential square: sit, enjoy, and keep voices low.

The church of Sant Joan closing the square, and how neighbours use it: benches, prams, terraces.
Where to listen
On a bench in the middle of the square, not on the church steps.
