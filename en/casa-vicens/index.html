Neighbourhood: Gràcia

Casa Vicens

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Stand back for a moment and take in the whole house. This was Antoni Gaudí's first important building, designed in the 1880s as a summer home for Manuel Vicens, a stockbroker, and finished in 1885. Back then Gràcia was still a separate town, surrounded by fields, and this plot was a country retreat away from the crowded city. Now let your eyes settle on the walls. You'll see bands of green and white tiles, and if you look closely, small yellow flowers painted on them. They're marigolds. Gaudí found them growing on the plot and turned them into decoration, so the flowers that once grew here still bloom on the house. Next, notice how the tiles sit alongside plain brick. Instead of hiding everything behind smooth stone, he let the materials show and stacked them in stripes, which gives the façade its lively rhythm. Look up toward the top of the house and you'll spot Moorish details, small turrets and decorative shapes that borrow from the Islamic architecture of Spain's past. Then glance at the iron railings: the fence is decorated with fan-shaped palm leaves, another piece of nature brought into the design. All of this shows a young architect already breaking the rules, years before his more famous works in the city. Today the house is a museum, and since 2005 it has been part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of Gaudí's works. The fields are long gone, and this is now a busy residential neighbourhood, so please keep the pavement clear for the people who live here.

Tip: It's on a narrow residential street: admire it from the pavement without blocking neighbours' doors.
