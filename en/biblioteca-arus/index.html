Biblioteca Pública Arús

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Look for the iron sign on the façade: Biblioteca Pública Arús. Behind this ordinary-looking building on Passeig de Sant Joan is a library born from a will. When the writer and freemason Rossend Arús died in 1891, he asked his executors to turn his own flat and his books into a library for the education of working people. It opened on 24 March 1895, with about twenty-four thousand volumes. Think of what that meant at the time: a free place to read for people who could not buy books, in a city full of factories and of new ideas about work, education and freedom. Today the library holds around sixty-nine thousand volumes and is a research centre on the workers' movement, anarchism and freemasonry, with newspapers and pamphlets that are hard to find anywhere else. It also keeps a large collection devoted to Sherlock Holmes. In the hall, at the end of the entrance, a small bronze replica of the Statue of Liberty welcomes readers. Its history has had silences too: after 1939 it was closed to the public and only reopened in 1967. Remember that it is still a working library: people come here to study.

Tip: It is a working library: listen out here, and inside keep silent and don't photograph readers.

What to look at
From the boulevard: the iron lamp-sign of the library on number 26. Inside, during opening hours: the wooden reading room and the small Statue of Liberty in the hall.
Where to listen
On the central walkway of Passeig de Sant Joan, opposite number 26, not in the doorway or on the stairs.
Visiting
Free entry to the public rooms during opening hours (closed in August). Guided visits run separately, with a ticket. Readers come first: silence inside.
