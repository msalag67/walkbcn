Palau Güell

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing on Carrer Nou de la Rambla, a narrow street just off the Rambla, facing one of Antoni Gaudí's early masterpieces. He built this mansion between 1886 and 1888 for his patron, the industrialist Eusebi Güell. Start with the two entrance arches. They're parabolic, a curve that carries weight smoothly down to the ground, and they're wide enough for horse-drawn carriages. Once inside, the carriages went down a ramp to stables in the basement. Now look closely at the iron gates in those arches. Can you spot the letters E and G worked into the metal? Those are Eusebi Güell's initials. Between the arches is the iron coat of arms of Catalonia, a proud sign of identity. Let your eyes travel up the facade. It's calm, almost severe, with straight lines and smooth stone, nothing like the swirling buildings Gaudí would design later. The drama is kept inside and above. The main hall rises to a dome pierced with small holes that let in light like stars. And up on the roof, out of sight from here, a forest of chimneys is covered in broken tiles, a first hint of the playful Gaudí to come. Today the building is part of a UNESCO World Heritage listing for Gaudí's work. People live on this street, so as you take it all in, please keep your voice low and leave room on the pavement.

Tip: Look up at the wrought ironwork over the doors: it's one of Gaudí's earliest masterpieces in iron.
