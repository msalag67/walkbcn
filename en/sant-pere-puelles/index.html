Neighbourhood: Sant Pere

Sant Pere de les Puel·les

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

This square, and the three streets of Sant Pere around it, take their name from the church in front of you. In the 10th century, Count Sunyer and Countess Riquilda founded a convent of Benedictine nuns here, outside the city walls, and its church was consecrated in 945. The nuns were known as puel·les, from the Latin word for young women. The convent shaped the whole area: for centuries, houses, gardens and workshops grew around it. Now, don't be fooled by the medieval look of the façade. In 985 the convent was attacked during al-Mansur's raid, and the count Borrell the Second restored it. A new, larger church was consecrated in the 12th century. In 1835 the nuns had to leave and the convent became a prison, and in 1879 the community moved to Sarrià, where their monastery still stands. The church burned in 1909, losing its old bell tower, and was damaged again during the Civil War. What you see is the result of all those repairs: very little of the first building survives, though a chapel inside keeps remains of an even older church. So read this façade as a building with many layers, not as a single moment in time.

Tip: Neighbours live all around this square and it is still a church: listen from the open side, away from the doors and steps.

What to look at
The plain stone façade and the octagonal bell tower. Compare them with the old photo below, taken before the 1909 fire.
Where to listen
On the open side of the square, near the fountain, leaving the church steps and the doorways of the houses clear.
Visiting
Best enjoyed from outside. The church opens for services; if you go in, do so quietly and not during Mass.
