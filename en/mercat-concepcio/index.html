Neighbourhood: Eixample

Mercat de la Concepció

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

You're standing at Mercat de la Concepció, one of the neighbourhood markets that keeps the Eixample fed. It opened in 1888, designed by the architect Antoni Rovira i Trias, and it has served the right side of the Eixample ever since. Before you go in, take a moment with the flower stalls outside. Their buckets and bunches spill colour right onto the pavement, and they're what this market is best known for. You may see people stop on their way past and pick up a bunch for the table as casually as a loaf of bread. Now look up at the building itself. The roof is iron and glass, the materials of its age, letting daylight fall across the counters below. Step inside and you'll find a working market rather than a showpiece. There's fruit stacked in careful piles, butchers at their counters, fresh fish laid out, and cheese stalls with wheels and wedges on display. Listen for a moment. Most of the conversations around you are between stallholders and regulars, and very few of the voices belong to tourists. That's what makes this place worth a visit. It's one of the best spots to see how people in the Eixample really shop. If you'd like to join in, buying a piece of fruit or a little cheese is a welcome way to do it. Otherwise, keep to the side of the aisles, give way to anyone carrying full bags, and ask before photographing stallholders. You're a guest in someone's everyday routine here.

Tip: Buy something if you linger: the stalls are people's livelihoods, not a museum.
