Neighbourhood: Gràcia

Nou Sardenya

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Barcelona has another historic football club, and you're standing at its home. This is Nou Sardenya, the ground of CE Europa. The name is easy to unpack: nou is Catalan for new, and Sardenya is the street the stadium sits on. Europa was founded right here in Gràcia in 1907, not long after Gràcia stopped being a town of its own and became part of Barcelona. That independent spirit still runs through the neighbourhood, and through this club. In 1923, Europa reached the final of the Spanish Cup. Then, in 1929, it played in Spain's very first national league, one of just ten clubs, alongside the biggest names in the country. Few people walking past today would guess that this modest ground belongs to one of the clubs from that very first season. Take a look at the stadium itself. It's small, and it's squeezed between apartment blocks so tightly that neighbours can almost watch matches from their balconies. Glance up and imagine a match day: the shouts from the stands, the flags, perhaps a resident leaning over the railing with a coffee, catching a free view of the game. There are no grand approaches here, just the ordinary streets of Gràcia running right up to the gates. Europa is a true neighbourhood club. Its loyal, passionate fans see it as part of Gràcia's identity, as much a part of local life as the squares and corner bars nearby. Remember, these are people's homes, so enjoy the view quietly before you move on.

Tip: If there's a home match, buying a ticket is a great way to see local football up close.
