Museu Picasso

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Picasso arrived in Barcelona in 1895, aged 13, and it was on these streets that he grew up as an artist. He'd been born in Málaga, and his father, an art teacher, took a post at the city's fine arts school, La Llotja, where the young Pablo enrolled too. Now look along Carrer Montcada. This narrow lane was where Barcelona's wealthy merchants once lived, and the heavy stone fronts and big doorways you see were built to show off that wealth. Five of these medieval palaces have been joined together to house the Museu Picasso, which opened in 1963. If you step inside, notice the courtyards behind the doors, with open stone staircases climbing to the main floor, a typical layout for the grand houses of this street. The collection is especially strong on Picasso's early years. You can follow him almost room by room, from careful academic studies to Science and Charity, a large, serious painting he made at fifteen, showing a doctor and a nun at a sick woman's bedside. It looks like the work of a seasoned professional. Then the rules start to slip. Another part of the collection holds his variations on Velázquez's Las Meninas, which Picasso himself gave to the museum. In them he takes one famous painting apart again and again. It's the clearest picture you'll get of a prodigy who learned to paint like an old master, then spent his life breaking everything he'd been taught. While you wait, keep your voice down: people live in the flats above these lanes.

Tip: Free entry on Thursday evenings and the first Sunday of the month (book ahead).
