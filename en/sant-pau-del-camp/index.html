Sant Pau del Camp

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Look at the small stone church in front of you: it is one of the oldest buildings in Barcelona. Its name, Sant Pau del Camp, means Saint Paul in the fields. When it was built, this was open country, outside the medieval walls, and the Raval around you did not exist yet. The monastery is documented from the 10th century. In 985, when al-Mansur's troops attacked Barcelona, it was sacked, and the monks rebuilt it from the end of the 11th century in the Romanesque style you see now: thick walls, small windows and very little decoration. Look closely at the doorway. The two capitals on its columns are much older than the church: they are marble pieces from Visigothic times, reused by the builders. Above them, Christ sits in majesty between Saint Peter and Saint Paul. Inside, a funerary inscription found in 1596 is traditionally linked to Count Guifré Borrell, who died in 911 and is considered the monastery's possible founder. Behind the church hides a small 13th-century cloister with lobed arches. The monks left in 1835, and in 1879 the building was declared a national monument. It is still a parish church today.

Tip: It is still a parish church: if you go in, avoid service times and keep your voice down.
Enjoy it from outsidePlace of worship: quiet, and no visits during services
What to look at
The doorway: two old marble capitals and, above them, Christ between Saint Peter and Saint Paul. Then the bell gable and the round window.
Where to listen
In the small garden beside the church (Jardins de Sant Pau del Camp), not on the doorway steps.
Visiting
Enjoy it from outside. The church and the 13th-century cloister open to visitors with a ticket at set times, which change with services: check before going.
