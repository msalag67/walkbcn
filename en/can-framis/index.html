Neighbourhood: Poblenou

Museu Can Framis

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Look at the brick buildings and the tall chimney behind them. This was the wool factory of the Framis family, from the days when Poblenou was so full of mills, smoke and workers' housing that people called it the Catalan Manchester. Most of those factories closed in the second half of the 20th century, and the old industrial land around you has been transformed into a district of offices, universities and housing. Can Framis is one of the factories that survived that change. The architect Jordi Badia, of BAAS, restored two of its three buildings and rebuilt the third in concrete, so old brick and new concrete now stand side by side. Notice how the new part does not try to imitate the old one. On 27 April 2009 it opened as a museum of the Fundació Vila Casas, created by the businessman and collector Antoni Vila Casas. Inside, about two hundred and fifty works show Catalan painting from the 1960s onwards, across three floors and more than thirty rooms, with space for temporary exhibitions too. By the entrance stands the sculpture Dell'Arte, by Jaume Plensa, given to the museum in 2012. A factory that once spun wool now shows how artists from Catalonia have seen the world.

Tip: The square in front is open to everyone; the museum itself has a ticket and opening hours.
Enjoy it from outside
What to look at
The restored brick buildings, the new concrete one and the factory chimney behind them; Plensa's sculpture by the entrance.
Where to listen
In the open square in front of the entrance.
Visiting
The outside can be enjoyed freely. The museum has a ticket and opening hours (closed on Mondays and in August): check the Fundació Vila Casas website.
