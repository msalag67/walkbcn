Neighbourhood: Gothic Quarter

Temple d'August

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Hidden inside a small courtyard on Carrer del Paradís stand four Roman columns, nearly two thousand years old. Before you go in, look down outside the door. Set into the pavement is a round millstone. It marks the top of Mont Tàber, a small hill that was the highest point of the Roman town of Barcino. You might not notice much of a climb, but this is where the Romans built their temple to the emperor Augustus. Now step inside, and keep your voice low. This courtyard belongs to the Centre Excursionista de Catalunya, a hiking and mountaineering club that still uses the building. Look up. The columns rise about nine metres, and their tops are carved in the Corinthian style, with curling acanthus leaves. Let your eyes run down the shafts and notice the long vertical grooves, called fluting, cut along their length. At the bottom, the columns rest on a raised stone base, part of the platform the temple stood on. Picture the whole building around you, with more columns like these holding up a roof and the little town spreading down the slopes on every side. Barcino's main square, its forum, lay just nearby, around today's Plaça Sant Jaume. For centuries, later houses grew up around these columns and hid them from view. Today they stand in a quiet corner of a living neighbourhood. Enjoy them, and leave the courtyard as calm as you found it.

Tip: Entry to the courtyard is free; step in quietly, it's a working building.
