Neighbourhood: Gràcia

Mercat de la Llibertat

▶ Listen in WalkBCNFree audio story, no account

Gràcia was its own town until 1897, and it still behaves like one. You can feel it in these narrow streets and little squares, and you can feel it most of all right here. This iron-and-glass market dates from 1888, and it's where the neighbourhood actually does its shopping. Take a moment to look up first. The iron frame and the glass let daylight fall across the stalls, so the whole hall feels open and bright, more like a covered square than a building. Then bring your eyes back down to the counters. Notice how the stallholders know their customers by name. You might catch a quick greeting, a question about family, a word about what's good today. These conversations happen while things are being weighed and wrapped, and nobody seems in a hurry. The market shares its name with the square it stands on, Plaça de la Llibertat, and llibertat is simply the Catalan word for freedom. You'll hear plenty of Catalan around you, which is the everyday language for many people here. Remember that this is a working market, not a museum, so keep to the side of the aisles and leave room for people with shopping bags and trolleys. If you do want to buy something, it's a lovely way to take part. Try some local cheese, or a paper cone of olives to nibble as you carry on walking. And when you reach the counter, start the way locals do, with a warm bon dia. It means good morning, and it opens doors.

Tip: Mornings are busiest with locals; keep the aisles clear and buy something if you linger.
